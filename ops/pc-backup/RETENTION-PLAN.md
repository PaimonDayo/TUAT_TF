# Backup inventory and dry-run retention candidate

`retention.mjs` exports pure `planBackupAdmission(input)`, `planBackupRetention(input)` and receipt/inventory reconciliation. It has no CLI, filesystem, environment, database or provider access and always returns dry-run decisions. The supervisor now connects its private receipt producer to bounded local and remote discovery/reverification, explicit capacity admission and `retention-dry-run.json`; tests use fake transports/private synthetic files. **Deletion is not connected**. No real archive, credential or provider request was used to test this candidate.

The caller supplies bounded flat JSON receipt metadata, explicit `identity` (`instanceId`, current `generation`, canonical UTC `startedAt`, `databaseSystemIdentifier`), `scope` (`targetFingerprint`, `storageScopeId`, `archiveKeyId`, each a 64-hex reviewed identity digest), the exact current `protectedPair`, a UUID `planNonce`, explicit millisecond `now`, `inventoryLimit` (1–4096) and `budgets`. Those digests must represent independently verified container/database, account/bucket and existing archive-key identities; self-asserted metadata does not prove a real target. Keep the existing namespace and archive key. Never put a key value in a receipt.

The proposed receipt contract is version 1 with `state:'verified'`, a UUID `receiptId`, the same instance/database/scope fields, its original lifecycle `generation`/`startedAt`, canonical UTC `capturedAt`/`verifiedAt`, exact `dumpKey`/`recoveryKey`, ciphertext SHA-256 values, nonempty `dumpEtag`/`recoveryEtag`, and `dumpBytes`/`recoveryBytes`. Keys must be the paired timestamp names under `ops/pc-backend/<instanceId>/backups/`. `localState` is `complete` or `absent`; `remoteState` is `verified` or `absent`. `localBytes`/`remoteBytes` must equal the ciphertext pair size when present and zero when known absent. Historical generations can support planning; only the exact supplied current-generation pair, present in both locations and verified less than 35 minutes ago, can be the protected pair. This does not replace the host's heartbeat/target/write guard.

Prepared, incomplete, uncertain, orphan, legacy and malformed records are held indefinitely and cannot become removal candidates. Optional known `localBytes`/`remoteBytes` on a held record are conservative usage reservations; missing sizes prevent a positive quota decision. Unknown extra/nested metadata and accessors are held without evaluating their payload and block all candidates because their object identities cannot be bound. A canonical future timestamp, conflicting verified scope, duplicate receipt/object identity (including a held attempt naming a verified object), changed/missing protected pair, excessive inventory or numeric overflow also blocks all candidates. A different legacy namespace remains held. It is never imported as a verified candidate merely because its filenames resemble new archives.

For verified pairs, the plan retains latest 24 local and latest 96 remote, plus the latest captured pair in each represented JST calendar day from today through the preceding 29 days. The current protected pair and latest two eligible pairs are always retained, including a protected pair captured before the other selected keepers but freshly verified. Fixed-count retention represents approximately 6/24 hours at ordinary 15-minute cadence; restarts can shorten that interval. It does not fabricate anchors for days without verified backups. Maximum selected verified sets are conservatively 55 local and 127 remote, plus all held objects.

`budgets` requires `maxLocalBytes`, `maxRemoteBytes`, `freeLocalBytes`, `minFreeLocalBytes`, `remoteBaselineBytes` (or explicit null when unknown), `nextPairBytes` (a conservative upper bound, at least 72), `maxRequestCount` and `maxTransferBytes`. Optional `nextLocalBytes`, `requestCount`, `transferBytes` carry the supervisor's explicit full-cycle estimates. All supplied usage/held reservations and the next pair are counted **before any candidate removal**. The connected reader accounts the entire dedicated local state and exact remote instance prefix, including unmatched/legacy objects as held usage; baseline outside that prefix is not counted or claimed. Missing usage or exceeded limits sets `writeAllowed:false`; this metadata decision is not an account fee guarantee. New dump/PUT stops at the explicit admission boundary; uncertain pairs are never purged to free space.

The runtime capacity config is one required block for enabled execution. `maxPairBytes` must be at least `128MiB + 6*(2*16MiB + 64KiB) + 64KiB + 72` (336,003,144 bytes). This derives the existing dump/SQL/Push adapter limits, worst-case JSON escaping, recovery metadata and two 36-byte TUATPC01 headers. Actual serialized plaintext and encrypted pair sizes are checked against these limits before local writes/PUT. The initial prepared intent reserves that pair maximum on local and remote storage. After exact sizes are known the reservation narrows, including for an unknown upload. A missing object never converts an unknown reservation to zero. A further 16MiB local reservation covers the bounded next receipt/dry-run manifest and status metadata; final serialized manifest must fit this allowance.

For `N` previously remote-verified same-scope receipts, `P` their total paired ciphertext bytes, `M=maxPairBytes`, `L=ceil(inventoryLimit/1000)+1`, `S=16MiB` per list page and `C=64KiB` per control response, the conservative per-cycle reservation is **`2L+8N+10` requests** and **`2P+3M+2LS+(8N+10)C` bytes**. It covers two complete listings, both rounds of old-pair HEAD/GET, the new pair's PUT/HEAD/GET and its final HEAD/GET. The initial listing is reserved before each request; the full allowance is checked before the first ciphertext GET, and each complete verification set is prepaid before its first HEAD/GET. There are no refunds or blind retries. These are per-cycle payload/control allowances, excluding HTTP/TLS framing, SDK overhead, DB probes and unrelated account use; they do not establish billing. A pathological/partial listing exceeding its page/entry allowance fails closed. Heartbeats never perform this inventory/readback work.

Output contains the identity/scope/nonce/time binding, digest of examined scalar inventory metadata, retained receipt references, exact paired removal candidates, held counts, quota estimates and a deterministic plan digest. Malformed payloads are represented by null in the inventory digest; their excluded content is not authenticated and no candidates are produced. A changed valid receipt, scope, nonce, budget or decision changes the plan digest. `planningAllowed`, `writeAllowed` and `removalCandidates` describe only this dry-run metadata calculation. They never authorize actual writes or deletion, never bypass host protection, and never establish actual quota or provider readiness. Unknown-size holds keep the quota decision unverified.

The supervisor uses the existing owner lock/target/scope/key and reserves before dump/PUT, then preserves the original at-capture receipt timestamp. It repeats the complete listing and actual local hash/remote readback and target probe before publishing a new protected pair. Initial admission has no protected pair and returns no removal candidates. After verification, the private dry-run manifest binds current identity, scope, nonce, time, receipt inventory/plan digest, full file/object proof digest and cycle budget with a full `manifestDigest`. Its exact pretty-JSON bytes and temporary write are capacity-checked before saving. A missing/changed current pair or incomplete inventory cannot publish new ready. A pure capacity overage may keep an already fresh same-generation pair only until its existing 35-minute expiry. The manifest is not an executable deletion approval.

Before any future remote removal, a separately reviewed owner must verify retained daily anchors, bind a fresh snapshot, recheck each exact object's ETag/size/hash and prove provider conditional-delete behavior. A partial/unknown DELETE must stop the batch without replay and preserve the local complete pair. Local removal needs protected paths and regular-file/inode/hash checks. None of those delete operations or old retention imports exist in this candidate.

Activation remains blocked on real target/account/key binding, prior writer/retention retirement, actual scoped storage/free-space and request/transfer budget values, quota/fee review and off-device recovery. The existing old-backend worker still uses its own retention; this candidate does not replace or stop it. A conflicting verified namespace/key in the dedicated state blocks planning until explicitly reconciled; no existing archive is moved, rekeyed or removed.

Synthetic acceptance tests: `node --test ops/pc-backup/{config,files,retention,adapters,run}.test.mjs` (expand the files explicitly on shells without brace expansion). They exercise admission versus selection, JST/current protections, private inventory, partial/missing/changed data, held reservations, quota before reclaim, upfront request/transfer reservation, overflow and bounded sequential I/O. Runtime tests use fake storage/dump adapters. They do not exercise real account inventory, deletion, fees or provider semantics.
